01

Grimsby / 1995–96

A band that disappeared.

Hovercraft formed in Grimsby in 1995. Songs were written, rehearsed, recorded onto tape and played while there was still a band around to play them. Then, in 1996, Hovercraft stopped.

The songs were Piers “Charlie Pepper” Wildman’s. He was a mod in the old devotional sense, raised on Quadrophenia and convinced, like Pete Townshend before him, that a three-minute song could carry the weight of a whole adolescence. Around him were Golly God on guitar, Ron Nasty and, eventually, Mr Shimble. The stage names were a joke, and also not.

The first rhythms came from a drum machine, Dr Rhythm, before teenage drummer “Dave” took over in October 1995. “Dave” played on Mr Tooting Brown, recorded that month at Pete’s Pad in Cleethorpes. When his parents put an end to his involvement, jazz drummer Vince Weaver stepped in at very short notice for the first Ropewalk gig on 9 February 1996. Daniel “Mr Shimble” Downing joined in March.

On Saturday 24 February 1996, Hovercraft were booked to play the Cartergate Rooms in Grimsby, on a bill with Spectral Noise, Lithium Joe and Curl. It was a Messcake Promotions affair, the creation of the band’s manager. The flyer survives. The gig never happened. As far as anyone remembers, it was Messcake’s first and last promotion.

See the Cartergate Rooms flyer ↗

Sean Belvoir-Hunter, alias Shaun K. Stokes, was the band’s manager. His surviving handwritten proposed contract assigned him the gigs, record deals and merchandising, for 20% of band earnings excluding royalties — with a majority-vote dismissal clause. The surviving copy has no signatures.

See the handwritten management agreement ↗

The County appearances in March and April were part of a Battle of the Bands competition. The band recalls reaching about the semi-final, where a sound engineer ruled out acoustic guitars for New Pine Overcoat. Piers was unimpressed. In May, Hovercraft recorded the six-song From The Cradle demo live, in one take without overdubs, at Chris McRae’s Sounds Good studio in Grimsby. Take It Or Leave It may have been omitted from some circulated copies.

The last gig was Spanish Steps in Cleethorpes on 22 June 1996. Later dates appeared on the June press release, but the band did not play them.

By then it was over. Pepper wanted the band to move to Brighton, and he was probably right. But England were hosting Euro 96, Gazza was performing miracles and Gareth Southgate was about to miss a penalty that would haunt the nation for decades. Golly and Ron had jobs. There were reasons to stay, or at least excuses not to leave.

Pepper left. The others and their manager carried on renting the vicar’s house they had shared since the summer of 1995, the house that had been the rehearsal room. It became a kind of unofficial free house until, sometime in 1997, they all went their separate ways. The band had lasted a few months in its final line-up, played a handful of gigs and recorded a six-song demo. The songs remained. The band did not.

The recordings survived. So did the songs — in different versions, on different tapes, and in the memories of the people who had made them.

02

The long gap

Nearly thirty years of absolutely nothing.

No masterplan. No carefully managed hiatus. Life happened, the band didn't, and the old Hovercraft material became archaeology.

What remained was unusually stubborn: songs that kept resurfacing in other recordings, other projects and eventually in a new set of experiments with the old material.

03

2025 / Return signal

Then somebody pressed transmit.

In 2025 the songs began escaping again — not as museum pieces, but as radically different new recordings. Singles became albums. Music blogs started writing about them. Hovercraft, inconveniently, existed again.

The original posts and outside reactions survive in sequence in the Transmission Archive.
