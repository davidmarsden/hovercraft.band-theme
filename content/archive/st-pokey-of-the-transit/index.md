**Hovercraft folklore · November 2026**

In 1996 Shaun K. Stokes, alias Sean Belvoir-Hunter, known to the band as Pokey, drew up a contract making himself Hovercraft's manager. It assigned him the gigs, the record deals and the merchandising, for 20% of band earnings excluding royalties. Nobody signed it. The gigs mostly happened, and the record deals never did.

Thirty years later, the merchandise finally arrived.

The promo CDs landed on Monday 2 November 2026: a slipcase, two discs, two booklets, "Thirty years in the making". By that evening the band WhatsApp had lit up.

"Dude, that's outstanding," said Ron. "Package looks amazing."

"Yeah they're fucking brilliant," said Piers, who wrote the songs and has now, officially, been sent them.

Gol offered to post copies to anyone who sent an address. Ron sent his, describing it as "alternatively known as the Centre of the Universe". Mr Shimble sent his from New South Wales and offered to chip in for postage. Gol said this one was on him, and asked whether a carrier pigeon would do. "Might be the furthest Hovercraft journey ever," said Mr Shimble.

Then Gol asked Pokey whether he'd like one.

He would not. He has no CD player and no address. He has gone back to living in the wild, in the woods, in his Transit van. He does have an acoustic guitar, so he's all good.

"Like St Francis of Assisi," said Piers. "But in a van."

"St Pokey of the Transit," said Pokey. "It was meant to be."

He admits he cheats: showers at work, and the facilities at the big Morrisons down the road. The van does have a CD player, but after nearly two years he still doesn't have the radio code. He thought about sorting it out, then chose silence instead, and found his true insanity on long journeys.

The merchandise has finally arrived, and the man who claimed it in 1996 is the one person who has turned it down. That saves Gol £4.50 in postage, and Pokey is the happiest man in the story. He won't get his four pound fifty either. Ask [Bob](/lyrics/bob-bob-bob/).
