**Hovercraft mythology · by Ron Nasty**

*The following anecdotes are a parody but not a wholly untruthful story of the real band Hovercraft, written by Nasty:*

The unlikely Hovercraft spawned from the bastard loins of alcoholism, gothic poetry and drug abuse during the death throes of a British summer, sometime in the mid 1990s.

Hailing from Grimechester and Spamhampton, Pepper, God, Nasty and Shimble wreaked havoc upon an unsuspecting and ill-prepared music scene still soaked in the putrid remnants of Stock, Aitken and Waterman’s shite pop of the Eighties and the similarly shite mullet cut Bon Joviesque hair rock of the same decade.

Steeped in the lore of Bakuninite philosophy, Gandhi’s satyagraha and Crowley’s ‘Majick’, the PreFab Four - fortified by a steady diet of badly cut speed, super strength lager, White Lightning, dope, milk and Boozy Bill’s hardcore pornography - forged an unholy partnership under the unerring but ultimately calamitous guidance of their self-proclaimed manager Sean Belvoir-Hunter.

Selling their souls to the MessCake Productions Company they entered the bowels of a Kafkaesque Hell to be spewed out nine months later, slightly better at Scrabble and vaguely altered by their experience, but victorious in their anarchy.

When asking Mr Nasty (now Nice) for his recollections of the band’s first meeting and time together he replied in an idiosyncratic tense:

“We met in the morning\
At the boozer by the station\
Where we drank to Che Guevara\
And the revolution that spawned him\
We stole the benefits put before us\
And threw up in the toilets\
Before walking to the corner\
Of the street bathed in the dusk\
Pepper’s house was rather slender\
He had a kettle and a toaster\
And there were holes in the ceiling\
Where the weather crept in\
We joined him in the parlour\
The needle scratched and smarted\
As we listened to Charlie Parker\
On a stereophonic record machine\
I wish we’d never kicked\
The door right off its hinges\
It was an inelegant display\
Of misplaced masculinity\
It ended before it started, and\
I’m sorry for the stains left on the carpet.\
I’d like to meet again, in the garden,\
Before the sun grows cold.”

Pepper, now existing within an experimental AI inspired alternative spacetime, merely snarled: “Penny! Who stole my feckin’ sausaygees!”

God, residing in a palatial marble-clad Londinium penthouse, surrounded by the paraphernalia of an illustrious solo recording career, exclaimed:

“Long live Hovercraft! All Hail Eris! Bill Hicks for President of the known multiverse! Belvoir-Hunter is innocent!”

Whilst humming: “I don’t want you for money, don’t want you for free, you gotta bring my baby back to me” (the opening lyric to an obscure but much sought after Hovercraft 4-track rarity), he lurched from his Ox Blood Chesterfield armchair to grasp a stained and aged glass chillum from the surface of a leather bound vintage Alexander Theodore scholars coffee table.

“Memories and details… hippocampus… temporal lobe… it’s indexed, filed and stored in there.”

“Unfortunately, accessing that information may stimulate the Amygdala which could potentially release a torrent of repressed memories and produce a potentially dangerous emotional response…”

“Wanna take a hit?” he said, passing the languidly smoking chillum.

Mr Shimble, the tubthumping antipodean arachnoid, reportedly attested to having no memory of Grimechester or Spamhampton but had lucid memories of Kafka and Scrabble. He maintained that Hovercraft was a figment of his imagination.

Belvoir-Hunter determined that he was unable to comment due to a long standing legal battle with Malcolm Malcolm of Slit&Brest Inc for the ownership of the fictitious “but observably real” ChilledOutLamppost record label, Fourplay Promotions and the MessCake Productions Company.

“It’s a right scrambled egg of a spaghetti junction,” he said, whilst placing a £50 bet on the nose of the renowned three-legged nag Sophie's Knee. A certainty for the Grand International, he observed.

“Now, where on earth did I put those sausages!?”

### Gammon: The Charred Remains

Light plumes of ever darkening smoke filtered into the lounge from the kitchen door, which had been left slightly ajar. The lads, too stoned to be aware of anything amiss, were crowded around the Monopoly board.

An argument had broken out over the amount of Income Tax due on Leo Rammerstein’s three hotels situated on Park Lane and the ensuing cost of repairs to the Water Works, due to a miscalculation of thrust and lack of turning circle on Mr Shimble’s Kurtis Kraft automobile.

The ensuing flood of Piccadilly and Coventry Street had angered Nasty; his boot had been soaked and the terraced housing had been ruined. Shimble denied culpability, accusing Boozy Bill of stealing the Kurtis and taking it for a joyride whilst high on Meth.

Belvoir-Hunter asserted that his bank could only loan out money for flood damage and housing repairs on a 23% compounded interest rate over a completion of 5 circuits of the board: “It all boils down to the Law of Fives,” he said.

There was also nothing Belvoir-Hunter could do to cover the repairs of the Kurtis automobile as Shimble had failed to pay his Motoring Insurance and the MoT was overdue.

Bill looked on morosely as the silver VHS Amstrad VCR-4600 chewed up *Big Tits Across Huddersfield*, his favourite Bluey.

God and Pepper, removed from the drama at the Monopoly board, were going at it hammer and tongs over a purported illegal chess move.

“There ‘ain’t no feckin’ thing called En Passant,” Pepper frothed from his thin lipped grin.

“En Passant, for your information, has been an official chess move since 1880,” explained God.

“There’s a lack of madness in your method, dear boy,” he mocked, removing the pawn with a dexterous flick of his wrist. Pepper flared his nostrils and picked furiously at his aquiline nose…

“Boys! Boys!” A female voice shrieked, piercing the atmosphere and silencing the squabbling.

“The oven’s ablaze!!”

Shimble abandoned the Kurtis in Free Parking and rushed towards the kitchen door as Nasty continued to remonstrate with Belvoir-Hunter over social inequality and the devastating effects of Neo-Liberalism on the world economy.

Boozy Bill, wiping beads of sweat from his brow, abandoned his furtive attempts to free *Big Tits* from the voracious mouth of the VCR, whilst Pepper threw himself back into the depths of his moth eaten armchair, legs crossed in resignation as God played his final move.

“The King has lost his crown, eh Pepper?”

Rammerstein, ever the opportunist, took his chance and stealthily Passed Go, grabbing a fistful of dollars from the vacated bank for good measure.

“Belvoir-Hunter, you need to see this,” shouted Shimble from the kitchen.

The remains of a shrivelled and charred joint of gammon sat sadly on the scorched counter top of the heavily smoke damaged kitchen. The once gleaming Frigidaire Flair oven was now covered in thick black soot and appeared to be way beyond repair.

Belvoir-Hunter stood amongst the carnage, prodding at the carcass with an elongated stainless steel meat fork, misappropriated from Gimbles & Gullets Butchery earlier that day:

“Sunday dinner’s fucked then,” he sighed.

### Crossroads

The band had completed the painstaking recording of its single song demo ‘Mr Tooting-Brown’ at Malcolm Malcolm’s swanky Riverthorpe music studio, ‘Idle Prophets & False Promises’, and had just taken delivery of the tape.

They gathered in the lounge for their first listen of the finished product on God’s proudly assembled Townshendesque “state-of-the-art quadrophonic sound system”.

An awesome piece of kit made up of four tatty looking Bush Arena Hi-Fi L820 teak surround speakers, hung from the ceiling in each corner of the room, powered by a black Technics RS-TR333 Double Cassette Deck.

God gently, but firmly, ushered Bill away from the prized tape player - conscious of the terrible VCR incident that had ruined Bill’s beloved blue movie - before carefully slotting the cassette into the cartridge, clicking the casing shut, turning it up to eleven and delicately pressing play.

A low hum of crackling white noise and static foreshadowed a cataclysmic explosion of guitars, drums and bass, sculpted via Malcolm Malcolm’s studio’s Marshall JCM 900 stack, 1972 Fender Twin Reverb and Fender Bassman. Pepper’s rasping, sneering, nasal vox cutting through the slow rock tempo wall of sound like a grater through cheese:

“Mr Tooting-Brown\
You’d better not look down…”

*[Read the full lyric of Mr Tooting Brown →](/lyrics/mr-tooting-brown/)*

Pepper, God and Nasty nodded in appreciation as the minor work of genius created by Malcolm Malcolm’s team of production engineers rocked the foundations of the urbane semi-detached residence.

“Nice feckin’ one!” shouted an elated Pepper as the track thundered towards its climax.

“Malcolm wants us to sign with him. We’ll only have to pay for studio time and he’ll take care of everything else… management, recording, marketing, distribution, gigs, tour costs,” said God.

“Mind blowing! I don’t want to throw a spanner in the works but we can’t even afford to buy a pack of fucking sausages, let alone pay Malcolm for studio time,” muttered Nasty.

The band looked at each other. Disappointment hung heavy in the air. They knew it was an impossible deal to broker.

Belvoir-Hunter, pulling out a sheaf of rolled up A4 sized documents from the back pocket of his Levi 501s, broke the silence: “Look, forget about Malcolm, Boozy Bill will sort you out with his old showbiz agent Cheap Pete, he’s sure to want to hire you.”

Bill nodded his head: “Yeah, brother Pete’s the man and you can jam at the Vinyl Tapes rehearsal place, down at the garages on Last Resort Street. Arlington the Hippy will do you a good deal, we go way back.”

Belvoir-Hunter smiled: “InBetween times we’ll run off copies of the demo on the Technics and drop them at the local pubs and clubs. Nobody’s gonna notice a reduction in sound quality and Malcolm’ll be none the wiser.”

“Nice one!” said God and Nasty in unison.

“That’s the spirit,” said Pepper. “Where do we sign?”

A beaming Belvoir-Hunter arranged the paperwork on the oversized rectangular pouffe which doubled as a coffee table.

“Just here…. erm… and here and, er, there…” Belvoir-Hunter said, jabbing at the paperwork.

“In blood,” he added, passing over a razor blade.

### The Library Of The Damned

The guys were down the pub, no doubt watching Belvoir-Hunter clean out the pockets of the regulars with his favoured hustle, killer pool. Meanwhile, in the relative tranquility of the empty house Nasty had taken the opportunity to finish reading Julian May’s *The Many-Coloured Land: The Saga of the Exiles*. He’d needed a little light sci-fi to recharge his batteries.

As he put the book down, the voracious reader’s eye was taken by the Vicar’s well-stocked bookshelves. He’d previously paid scant interest in them but now, now he spied, amongst the expected religious tomes, crucifixes and other reverent artefacts, several surprising titles:

Albert Camus, *The Outsider*; Jean-Paul Sartre, *No Exit*; Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson, *The Illuminatus! Trilogy*; Arthur Miller, *The Crucible*; Friedrich Wilhelm Nietzsche, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*; Leo Tolstoy, *War and Peace*; Carl Sagan, *Cosmos*; Cassia Joy Cowley, *The Ghost and the Sausage*; Robert Silverberg, *The Conglomeroid Cocktail Party*; Aleister Crowley, *The Book of Thoth*.

“Curious,” mused Nasty, “that’s not the reading material I’d associate with a man of the cloth.”

“They’re mine,” boomed a dark, looming, shadowy figure, silhouetted against the kitchen doorway.

“Jesus!” Nasty exclaimed, nearly jumping out of his skin.

“Not Jesus, God,” laughed God.

“Help yourself, I’ve got more literature stowed away in my room.”

Nasty lightly fingered the titles before coming to rest on *The Illuminatus! Trilogy*, tucked alongside *Capability Brown and the Landscape Gardener* and propped up against *The Chess Player's Bible*.

“That’s a good choice,” said God. “We’ve got rehearsal in an hour Nasty, let’s get the car loaded.”

### Bummer! No Drummer

The proposed management and recording deal with Malcolm Malcolm was dead in the water and to make matters worse original Hovercraft drummer, Young Dave Somebody, hadn’t been in touch since an awkward meeting between the band and his parents outside Arlington the Hippy’s rehearsal studio, Vinyl Tapes - itself a shit pit of flaky plaster and filthy carpets, which sat in stark contrast to Malcolm Malcolm’s clean, bright, hygienic and hi-tech facility at Idol Prophets & False Promises.

The lack of a drummer had become a real concern. Nasty and God knew Shimble’s associate Leo Rammerstein could bash the bongos but he appeared either disinterested in the opportunity or too stoned to be bothered.

They were now a band mostly drawing on state benefits, squatting in a Vicar’s Victorian semi-detached house, with no drummer. And, to top it off, a series of gigs were just weeks away. It was adding up to be a disaster of some magnitude.

“I’ll do it,” announced Shimble, dextrously twirling a set of sticks between his fingers.

“When did you learn to play the fucking drums?” Nasty enquired.

“I read the books and I’ve heard all your material,” replied Shimble, tossing well worn copies of *Stick Control For The Snare Drummer* by George Lawrence Stone and *Progressive Steps To Syncopation For The Modern Drummer* by Ted Reed onto the scorched kitchen counter top.

“It may also have escaped your attention but I spent hours thrashing around on the kit in the basement when you were twatting around with Dr Rhythm in your last band, No Fit State.”

“Better yet, my compensation’s just come through,” he grinned.

Shimble’s stint in the REME had been marred by his arthritic knees, making it impossible for him to square bash. They had him out of there within months of taking the King’s shilling.

“It’s a fucking miracle! We’d better get you a kit then,” said Nasty.

A road trip to Oddcaster in Nasty’s Vauxhall Astra 1.8 SRi and a visit to the infamous Oedipus & Electra music store, purveyors of custom contrivances for musical composition.

After entertaining the customers, and surprising Nasty and the store’s drum salesman with his skills, Shimble left with a gleaming five-piece set of white Premier drums and hardware.

Henceforth, Mr Arachnid-Wan-Wan-Wan-Shimble-Gremalkin became the newest member of Hovercraft.

### Boogie Chillum

Constant consumption of class A drugs, super strength alcohol and foraged cocktails of hallucinogenic fungi, combined with the recent traumatic events of band life, left Hovercraft on the verge of burnout and suffering a bad case of the shits.

The gigs had been explosive; love them or loathe them, there wasn’t a band in the area that could compete with Pepper’s songwriting, vocal delivery or swagger. Hovercraft's raw energy and stage presence was as oddly terrifying as it was addictive; it truly was “cataclysmic rock ‘n’ roll of the most intense order”.

Shimble had been a revelation, amazing and delighting his bandmates and audiences with his otherworldly drumming. It may be an over observation, but Keith Moon, John Bonham, Ginger Baker, Bill Ward - who the fuck were they in comparison to the eight-legged groove machine driving the rhythm section of the hottest act in Grimechester?

Near exhausted and due a break, the band convened in the lounge. They’d been awake for days snorting, dabbing and bombing speed, hitting the pubs and clubs, picking up randoms, attending groupies and other bands' parties and generally getting fucked up to a greater degree than usual. Nasty had even quit his job as a production journalist due to the intensity of Hovercraft life.

“It’s time to get off the trip, man!” Shimble announced, as he sat cross-legged with Boo, his black cat, purring contentedly in his lap.

Using a heavyweight vintage King James Bible, pilfered from the bookcase, and a Scrabble board as a platform, he carefully placed a battered Old Holborn tobacco tin containing an eighth of squidgy black, a smattering of green, a pack of super-sized Rizla and an essential refillable Clipper lighter.

For good measure, he produced a glass Chillum, a dinner knife, several pins and a glass tumbler: “Just in case we want to get funky with the crumbly,” he explained. Nasty threw a 10 pack of Embassy No. 1 cigarettes into the mix and before long Shimble had produced a doobie the size of Manhattan.

“Let’s chill, man… get creative.”

The doobie did the rounds, followed by another. Belvoir-Hunter, God and Nasty began exorcising the Chillum and Pepper, a habitual speed freak who’d just consumed a full pack of Nasty’s ProPlus tablets, finally relented and began hot knifing the bud.

“Nice,” said Pepper, “but I’ve got something much better.”

He reached for God’s semi-acoustic guitar and strummed a beautifully melodic extended guitar intro.

“It’s called ‘New Pine Overcoat’,” he said, breaking into the vocals:

### The Special Brew Session

The band rolled in for its recording session at Vinyl Tapes. Arlington met them in the control room and laid down the law: “No drugs, no bullshit, six songs back to back absolutely live, no click, no overdubs, absolutely no fucking drugs.”

A contrite Hovercraft agreed to Arlington’s terms. Pepper gave him a hug and God smiled warmly, putting him at his ease. Nasty and Shimble made a few positive and friendly gestures and promised to behave before heading into the studio and setting their gear up.

A case of Special Brew, a strong alcoholic lager (ABV 7.5%) with the consistency of treacle, mysteriously appeared behind the drum kit. Pepper winked, Arlington pretended not to notice. The session was on.

The boys were so well rehearsed it took very little time to lay the tracks down and there were minimal issues during the recording process - a broken guitar string on *Concrete Hill* was ignored, Pepper improvising to keep the vibe going, and a broken bass string on *White Limousine* was quickly resolved.

The resultant six-track demo EP, initially mixed by Nasty, Pepper and Arlington and later re-mixed by God and Arlington, was entitled *From The Cradle…. To The Grave*, and listed the following songs:

- Indie Kid

- New Pine Overcoat

- Concrete Hill

- White Limousine

- To The Grave

- Take It Or Leave It

### New Band Hovers In

The demo found its way to the local press and the resultant article “New Band Hovers In” reads:

> Look out for new Grimechester band Hovercraft who play UnLimited in Riverthorpe on Wednesday, and also have a date at Grimechester’s Country Hotel on Sunday, August 4th.
>
> I have to say the band’s six-track demo tape is a real scorcher… the music mixes elements of sound from the last three decades with an added pinch of the blues…

Weeks later Hovercraft ceased to exist.
