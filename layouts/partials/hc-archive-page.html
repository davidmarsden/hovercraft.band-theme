1995–96 / Original band

Before the
transmissions.

Contemporary press releases document the gigs; later recollections supply the stories. Dates below are documented performances, with the competition round and personnel details qualified where remembered.

  • 9 February 1996 — The Ropewalk · Vince Weaver deputised at short notice; first Ropewalk gig
  • 3 March 1996 — The County · Battle of the Bands
  • 31 March 1996 — The County · Battle of the Bands
  • 21 April 1996 — The County · Battle of the Bands; likely semi-final, acoustic-guitar dispute recalled
  • 25 April 1996 — Lloyds · Listed in June 1996 press release
  • 10 May 1996 — The Ropewalk · Listed in June 1996 press release
  • 11 May 1996 — The Hainton · Listed in June 1996 press release
  • 22 June 1996 — Spanish Steps, Cleethorpes · Final Hovercraft gig

Advertised, not performed: Limits, Cleethorpes (3 July, TBC), and The County, Grimsby (4 August). Spanish Steps was the final gig.

In October 1995 “Dave” replaced Dr Rhythm and recorded Mr Tooting Brown at Pete’s Pad. May 1996 brought the six-song From The Cradle demo, recorded live without overdubs at Chris McRae’s Sounds Good studio.

Original sources include the June 1996 press release, Introducing Hovercraft, Steve Jackson’s South Bank Show clipping, and the local Demo Top Ten. See the original scanned press release and clippings and visual archive.

2009—now / After the band

Other lives,
other songs.

Piers. In Bournemouth, as Boscombe Philosophical Orchestra, Piers recorded his own versions of songs now in the Songbook, among them To The Grave, I Love You (And I Don't Want You To Die), Concrete Hill and Superman, alongside songs of his own from after the band. From 2010 to 2016 he also wrote Not Too Likely, a blog of stories, essays and the serial Dwarf House. Some of it starts from real life, and some of it stars a character called Charlie Pepper, but as his disclaimer puts it: “what I write is NOT the truth.”

  • Boscombe Philosophical Orchestra on Bandcamp
  • Piers Wildman on SoundCloud
  • Not Too Likely (writing)

Gol. David Marsden writes about politics, football, food and family, and in July 2025 wrote about digging out the lost Hovercraft tapes. His album Algorithm + Blues includes Here Now, a song Piers gave him in 2009. By day he works for a community charity in Southall, West London.

  • Writing
  • Music

Ron Nasty. After the dissertation, Aaron spent much of his working life as a newspaper sub-editor. These days he's a gardener in Lincolnshire, and he still plays bass in local covers bands.

Mr Shimble. Dan emigrated to Australia, where he has spent more than twenty-five years working in IT and information security. That pretty much rules out a reunion tour, or another attempt at playing the Cartergate Rooms.
